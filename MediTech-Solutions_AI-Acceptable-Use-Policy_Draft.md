## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the rules and expectations for the use of generative artificial intelligence tools at MediTech Solutions. Generative AI tools are applications that create text, code, images, audio, or other content based on user prompts. Examples include public chatbots, coding assistants, image generators, transcription services, and any other platform that processes user inputs to generate novel outputs.

The purpose of this policy is to enable responsible and productive use of generative AI while protecting MediTech patients, employees, intellectual property, and regulatory standing. MediTech recognises that generative AI offers genuine benefits in productivity, creativity, and decision support. This policy is not a prohibition. It is a framework for safe adoption.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies without exception to all individuals who access MediTech systems, handle MediTech data, or perform work on behalf of MediTech. This includes the following groups.

Permanent employees of MediTech Solutions, regardless of seniority, department, or location. Fixed term contractors and consultants engaged by MediTech. Temporary agency workers and locum staff. Third party representatives, vendors, and partners who have been granted access to MediTech information systems or who process MediTech data under a contractual agreement. Any other individual who uses a MediTech issued device, a MediTech network connection, or a MediTech software licence.

This policy covers all devices used for MediTech business. This includes company issued laptops, desktops, and mobile phones. It also includes personally owned devices when used for work purposes under a Bring Your Own Device arrangement or on an ad hoc basis. It covers all browsers, applications, and application programming interface integrations, regardless of whether the tool is accessed via the public internet or through an enterprise gateway.

Use of generative AI tools for purely personal, non work purposes on personal devices, outside working hours, and without any connection to MediTech systems or data is outside the scope of this policy. However, if any MediTech data is involved, the activity is in scope regardless of the device, time, or location.

---

## 3. The Core Principle

MediTech adopts a clear and simple core principle for generative AI use.

**Treat public generative AI tools as if you are speaking into a microphone in a public place. Anything you type can be stored, reviewed, processed, and potentially surfaced elsewhere. Do not share anything you would not be comfortable reading on the front page of a newspaper or hearing quoted in a regulatory hearing.**

Public generative AI tools are not private. They are not confidential. They are not secure environments for MediTech information. The provider of the tool may store your inputs. The provider may use your inputs to train future models. The provider may employ human reviewers to read and assess conversation logs. The provider may be compelled to disclose data under a legal request from a foreign jurisdiction. You have no control over what happens to your data once it leaves your browser.

Before typing anything into a generative AI tool, pause and ask yourself this question: "If this exact text appeared in a public forum tomorrow, would I be confident defending my decision to enter it?" If the answer is no, do not enter it.

## 4. Definitions

For clarity, the following definitions apply throughout this policy.

**Generative AI Tool:** Any software application, platform, or service that uses machine learning models to generate novel content in response to user prompts. This includes, but is not limited to, large language model chatbots, text to image generators, AI coding assistants, AI transcription and summarisation services, AI video and audio generation tools, and any application that embeds these capabilities.

**Public Generative AI Tool:** A generative AI tool that is freely available to the general public and has not been procured, contracted, or approved by MediTech IT and Legal departments. These tools typically have terms of service that grant the provider broad rights over user submitted data and offer no contractual protections for MediTech.

**Approved Enterprise AI Tool:** A generative AI tool that has been formally assessed by MediTech IT Security, Legal, and Procurement, has a signed data processing agreement in place, has contractual terms that prohibit the vendor from using MediTech data for model training, and is listed in the Approved Enterprise AI Tools register maintained by IT.

**Patient Identifiable Information:** Any information that could identify a patient, either directly or indirectly. This includes, but is not limited to, full name, any part of a name combined with other details, address including postcode, date of birth, NHS number, hospital patient record number, medical record number, any clinical notes or referral letters, scan images and reports, appointment dates and times, and any other unique identifier. Even a combination of non unique details, such as a rare condition and a geographical area, can be identifying. If there is any doubt, treat the information as patient identifiable.

**Company Confidential Information:** Any information that is not intended for public disclosure and whose unauthorised release could cause harm to MediTech. This includes pre patent research and development data, proprietary algorithms, trade secrets, internal financial reports, forecasts, and projections, merger and acquisition discussions, board papers and committee minutes, internal audit reports, legal advice and case strategy, and any document or data marked "Internal" or "Confidential" under MediTech's Information Classification and Handling Policy.

**Hallucination:** A known and documented limitation of generative AI in which the tool produces content that is factually incorrect, entirely fabricated, or contradictory to the source material, while presenting it with apparent confidence and plausibility. Hallucination is not a rare edge case. It is an inherent characteristic of current generative AI technology.

**Data Processing Agreement (DPA):** A legally binding contract between MediTech and a third party service provider that governs how the provider may process MediTech data. A DPA is required under UK GDPR whenever a third party processes personal data on MediTech's behalf.

## 5. What You Must Never Do

The following actions are strictly prohibited when using any public generative AI tool. This list is not exhaustive. If you are uncertain whether an action is permitted, seek guidance from your manager or the Privacy Team before proceeding.

**5.1 Patient Identifiable Information**

You must never enter patient identifiable information into a public generative AI tool. This prohibition is absolute and has no exceptions. Examples of prohibited behaviour include pasting a referral letter into a tool to summarise it, typing a patient's name and date of birth to generate a draft clinic letter, uploading a scan report to ask the AI to explain the findings in plain English, entering an NHS number to check formatting, and dictating case notes into a free transcription tool that processes audio via a public AI service.

De identification of patient data is a complex task that requires formal training and approved tools. Removing a name is not sufficient. A patient can be re identified from postcode, date of birth, rare conditions, unique combinations of medications, or the specific details of their clinical history. Do not attempt to de identify patient data yourself for the purpose of entering it into an AI tool. Use only approved, validated de identification processes.

**5.2 Company Confidential Information**

You must never enter company confidential information into a public generative AI tool. Examples include proprietary research data, algorithm code that has not yet been patented, internal financial figures, draft earnings reports, strategic planning documents, board meeting materials, and any document stamped "Internal" or "Confidential."

If you are working on a patentable invention, entering any part of the invention into a public AI tool may constitute public disclosure and can invalidate patent protection. This is not a hypothetical risk. It is established intellectual property law.

**5.3 Third Party Confidential Information**

You must never enter information received from a partner, supplier, client, or other third party under a non disclosure agreement, data sharing agreement, or other contractual confidentiality obligation. The fact that the information is not MediTech's does not reduce your obligation. MediTech is contractually and legally bound to protect it.

**5.4 Employee Personal Data**

You must never enter personal data about a colleague into a public generative AI tool. This includes performance review content, salary or compensation details, disciplinary notes, health information, absence records, and any other human resources data. Colleagues have the same data protection rights as patients.

**5.5 Authentication Credentials**

You must never enter any authentication credential into a generative AI tool. This includes passwords, passphrases, PINs, API keys, access tokens, encryption keys, multi factor authentication recovery codes, and any other secret used to prove identity or authorise access. Generative AI tools are not password managers. There is no legitimate business reason to share a credential with an AI.

**5.6 System Configuration and Security Information**

You must never share internal network diagrams, firewall rules, server configurations, vulnerability scan results, penetration test findings, or security incident details with a public AI tool. This information is of high value to attackers and its disclosure could directly facilitate a breach.

**5.7 Legal Advice and Privileged Material**

You must never use a public AI tool to draft or review legally privileged communications. Legal professional privilege belongs to MediTech and can be waived by sharing the material with a third party. A public AI platform is a third party. Waiving privilege can have severe consequences in litigation.

## 6. What Is Permitted

MediTech does not impose a blanket ban on generative AI. You are permitted to use public generative AI tools for general business tasks that do not involve sensitive, confidential, or personal data. The following examples are illustrative and not exhaustive.

Drafting an agenda for a routine team meeting that contains only logistical details and no patient, financial, or strategic content. Summarising a publicly available research paper or industry article that you are reading to stay current in your field. Rewording a non sensitive email about a social event, a team building activity, or an office announcement. Generating ideas for presentation slide titles where the slides will contain only publicly known information. Checking the grammar and clarity of a document that contains no sensitive content. Translating a publicly available text into another language for your own understanding.

The key test is simple. If your prompt contains only generic, non sensitive content, and you have no reason to believe the output will expose or derive protected information, the use is likely acceptable. If you are uncertain, stop and check with your manager or the Privacy Team. It is always better to ask before entering data than to report a breach after the fact.

## 8. Review and Verification of AI Outputs

You are accountable for the content you produce, regardless of whether an AI tool assisted in creating it. The AI tool is a productivity aid. It is not a source of truth. It is not a subject matter expert. It is not a delegated authority.

Generative AI tools are known to hallucinate. They can produce content that is factually incorrect, entirely fabricated, or internally inconsistent, while presenting it with confidence and plausible language. This is not a bug. It is a characteristic of the technology in its current state.

Before you use any AI generated content in your work, you must personally verify its accuracy against a reliable, human authored source. If the content relates to a clinical matter, a regulatory filing, a financial statement, a legal document, or any other domain where factual accuracy is critical, verification is mandatory and must be documented. You must be able to show your reviewer or auditor the source you used to confirm the AI output.

Never present AI generated content as your own unaided work if your role requires you to exercise professional judgement. This applies particularly, but not exclusively, to clinical staff, financial controllers, legal counsel, and anyone making decisions that affect patient care, financial reporting, or regulatory compliance.

If you use AI generated content in a document, presentation, or communication, consider whether it is appropriate to disclose that AI assistance was used. In some contexts, such as academic work or formal reports, disclosure may be required by professional standards. In others, it is a matter of transparency and good practice. If you are unsure, consult your line manager.

## 9. Data Retention and Disposal

Do not use public generative AI tools as a storage mechanism for MediTech information. Conversation histories stored in public AI platforms are not backed up by MediTech. They are not subject to MediTech's retention schedule. They are not discoverable in response to a data subject access request. They are not under MediTech's control.

Where an approved enterprise AI tool stores conversation history, that history is subject to MediTech's data retention policies. Do not assume it will be retained indefinitely. Do not assume it will be deleted on your instruction. Familiarise yourself with the specific retention rules for each approved tool.

If you need to keep a record of an AI interaction for audit or reference purposes, copy the relevant content into an approved MediTech document management system. Do not rely on the AI tool's own history feature.

## 10. Reporting an Accidental Disclosure

If you accidentally enter protected data into a public generative AI tool, take the following steps immediately.

First, stop what you are doing. Do not continue entering data. Do not attempt to delete the prompt history yourself. In many public AI tools, deletion is not possible or is not guaranteed. Attempting to delete data may create confusion about what was disclosed and when.

Second, report the incident to the IT Service Desk or the Privacy Team within one hour of becoming aware of it. Contact details are available on the intranet. If it is outside business hours, leave a message and send an email. The one hour clock starts when you become aware, not when the incident occurred.

Third, when you report, provide the name of the AI tool, the approximate date and time of the disclosure, and a general description of the type of data that was entered. Do not include the actual sensitive data in your initial report unless specifically asked to do so by the Privacy Team. For example, say "I pasted a referral letter containing a patient name and NHS number" rather than pasting the actual name and number into the incident ticket.

Fourth, cooperate with any follow up investigation. The Privacy Team may need to conduct a risk assessment, notify affected individuals, or report the incident to the Information Commissioner's Office. Your honest and timely cooperation is essential.

Reporting an honest mistake will not result in disciplinary action. This is MediTech policy and is stated explicitly here so there is no ambiguity. MediTech operates a just culture in relation to data protection incidents. We recognise that mistakes happen in a complex working environment. Failing to report a known disclosure is a separate and more serious matter. Deliberate concealment of a data breach is a disciplinary offence and may result in action up to and including dismissal.

## 11. Procurement and Shadow IT

No generative AI tool may be procured, deployed, or used for MediTech business without going through the formal IT procurement and security review process. This applies regardless of cost, including free tools, freemium tools, and tools paid for by personal credit card and expensed.

Shadow IT, which is the use of software and services without IT department knowledge and approval, is a significant risk to MediTech. It bypasses security review. It bypasses legal review. It bypasses data protection impact assessment. It creates data flows that are invisible to the organisation. A tool that appears harmless may have terms of service that are incompatible with MediTech's regulatory obligations.

If you identify a generative AI tool that you believe would benefit your team, submit a request through the procurement portal. The Privacy Team and IT Security will assess it. If the tool meets MediTech's standards, it will be added to the approved list. This process exists to enable safe adoption, not to block innovation.

## 12. Training and Awareness

All employees, contractors, and covered individuals must complete the mandatory Generative AI and Data Protection training module within 30 days of this policy taking effect, and annually thereafter. The training is assigned via the Learning Management System.

Line managers are responsible for ensuring their team members complete the training on time. Completion rates are reported to the Information Governance Committee quarterly.

In addition to the mandatory module, the Privacy Team maintains a set of resources on the intranet, including frequently asked questions, short video guides, and real world case studies. These resources are available to everyone and are recommended as part of ongoing awareness.

## 13. Monitoring and Compliance

MediTech reserves the right to monitor compliance with this policy through technical and administrative means. This may include network traffic analysis to detect the use of known public AI services, review of corporate device activity where permitted by policy and law, and audit of approved enterprise AI tool usage logs.

Monitoring is conducted in accordance with MediTech's Employee Privacy Notice and applicable employment law. Any monitoring is proportionate, risk based, and focused on detecting patterns of non compliance rather than individual infractions.

Identified violations of this policy will be addressed through the relevant disciplinary procedure. The specific response will depend on the nature, severity, and deliberateness of the violation, and whether it is a first offence or a repeated pattern. Sanctions may range from a documented conversation with a line manager to formal disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment or contract.

In cases involving a personal data breach, the incident will additionally be handled under the Data Protection Incident Response Procedure, which may involve external notification to regulators or affected individuals.

## 14. Reporting Concerns and Asking Questions

If you have a question about this policy, about a specific generative AI tool, or about a situation you are unsure how to handle, you have several options.

You may speak to your line manager in the first instance. You may contact the Privacy Team directly via the email address published on the intranet. You may raise a query through the IT Service Desk portal. You may use the confidential reporting line if you prefer to raise a concern anonymously.

No question is too small. No concern is unwelcome. The Privacy Team would rather answer ten precautionary questions than manage one preventable data breach.
